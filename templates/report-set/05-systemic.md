# Systemic themes

> Twenty findings that share one root cause are worth more than twenty findings. This is
> where an audit stops being a list and becomes an order of work, and it is what the summary
> leads with.
>
> A theme belongs here when fixing one thing closes several findings.

## [S-1] <the cause, stated as a cause>

- **Findings it explains:** `ID`, `ID`, `ID` … (n total)
- **Root cause:** what is actually missing or wrong, once, upstream
- **Why it reaches so far:** the mechanism
- **Fix once:** what to build or change, and where
- **What closes automatically:** which of the listed findings then need no separate work
- **What still needs individual work:** be honest — usually some do
- **Effort:** S/M/L · **Findings closed per unit of effort:** n

## Ranking

| Theme | Findings closed | Effort | Order |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |

Systemic ranks above pointwise even at equal severity, because it is paid for once. A plan
that starts at the top of this table and a plan that starts with the single
highest-severity finding are usually different plans, and this one is cheaper.

## Pointwise findings that survive

Findings that genuinely stand alone, after the themes above have absorbed everything they
explain. If this list is still most of the report, the synthesis was not done.
